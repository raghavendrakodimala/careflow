### 1. Purpose and Scope

1.1 This SOP defines how intake staff capture information, assign an urgency band, and document the first contact from a patient or a person calling on a patient's behalf.

1.2 It applies to every first contact by telephone, web form, patient portal message, walk-in, or fax/referral letter, whether the patient is new or established and contacting about a new issue.

1.3 It applies to all intake coordinators, front-desk staff, and Nurse Line call handlers. Clinical triage nurses are bound by clauses 4.6 to 4.9 only.

1.4 This SOP does not replace clinical judgment. Where this document and a clinician's direct instruction conflict, the clinician's instruction prevails and the conflict is recorded under clause 7.6.

---

### 2. Definitions

2.1 **First contact**: the first communication in which a patient or representative asks for care, advice, or an appointment about a specific issue.

2.2 **Intake record**: the electronic entry created in the Meridian Patient Access System (MPAS) under form code INT-01.

2.3 **Response**: a live, two-way contact by a clinician or trained intake coordinator that acknowledges the specific issue and states the next step. Auto-replies, voicemail messages, and "we received your request" texts are not a response.

2.4 **Clock start**: the timestamp on the intake record at the moment the contact began. For voicemails and web forms, it is the time the message was received.

2.5 **Duty Clinical Lead (DCL)**: the registered clinician rostered at each site, or for the Nurse Line, to take escalations.

---

### 3. Mandatory Fields Captured on First Contact

3.1 The following 12 fields must be completed before the intake record can be saved. Fields 1 to 8 are hard-stop fields. MPAS will not save the record if any is blank.

| # | Field | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Date and time of contact | Auto-stamped; 24-hour format; not editable |
| 2 | Contact channel | Select one: Phone, Web, Portal, Walk-in, Fax/Referral |
| 3 | Intake staff ID | Auto-filled from login; shared logins are prohibited |
| 4 | Patient full legal name | As on photo ID; no nicknames in this field |
| 5 | Date of birth | DD/MM/YYYY; confirm verbally by reading it back |
| 6 | Best callback number | 10 digits; read back to the caller |
| 7 | Reason for contact | Minimum one full sentence in the caller's own words; quote trigger phrases exactly |
| 8 | Urgency band | Routine, Priority, Urgent, or Emergent, per Section 4 |
| 9 | Symptom onset | Date, or "unknown" if the caller cannot say |
| 10 | Preferred site and specialty | Select from the MPAS list; default is nearest site |
| 11 | Insurance status | Select one: Provided, Declined (INS-DECL), Uninsured, Unknown. See Section 5 |
| 12 | Permission to leave detailed voicemail | Yes or No |

3.2 Staff must also ask, and record where given: interpreter need and language; emergency contact name and number; and referring clinician name. These three are collected on a best-effort basis. A blank entry does not block saving and must be marked "Not given" rather than left empty.

3.3 If the caller is not the patient, record the caller's name, relationship, and whether the patient has authorised the caller to discuss their care. If authorisation is not confirmed, disclose no clinical information and record "Authorisation not confirmed."

3.4 For a patient under 18, record the parent or guardian's name and callback number in addition to fields 4 to 6.

3.5 Intake staff must not ask for a Social Security number at first contact.

---

### 4. Urgency Banding

4.1 Every intake record is assigned exactly one band before the contact ends or, for asynchronous contacts, within 10 minutes of receipt.

4.2 The band is assigned by matching the caller's words to the trigger phrases below. Staff must record the specific phrase that triggered the band in field 7.

4.3 **Highest band wins.** If phrases from more than one band are present, assign the highest.

4.4 **Upgrade on doubt.** If staff are unsure between two bands, assign the higher and tell the DCL.

4.5 **Intake staff may never downgrade a band.** Only a registered triage nurse or physician may lower a band, and the change must be recorded with name, time, and reason.

#### 4.6 Band definitions, trigger phrases, and first-response targets

| Band | Target first response | Trigger phrases (exact or close paraphrase) |
|---|---|---|
| **Emergent** | **Immediately** | "chest pain" or "pressure in my chest"; "can't breathe" or "struggling to breathe"; "face drooping"; "slurred speech"; "sudden weakness on one side"; "unresponsive" or "won't wake up"; "seizure" (in progress or lasting over 5 minutes); "throat closing" or "lips swelling" after exposure; "bleeding that won't stop"; "overdose" or "took too many"; "swallowed" a poison or chemical; "want to end my life" or "going to kill myself"; "sudden loss of vision" |
| **Urgent** | **within 1 hours** | "fever over 103" (adult); any fever in an infant under 3 months; "can't keep fluids down" for 12 hours or more; "pain 8 out of 10" or higher; "blood in my urine"; "blood in my stool"; "wound opened up" or "stitches came out"; "pus" from a surgical site; "one leg swollen and painful"; "broke" or "think it's broken" with visible deformity; "fell and hit my head" (no loss of consciousness); "ran out of insulin" |
| **Priority** | **within 4 hours** | "getting worse" over 3 days or more; "pain 5, 6, or 7 out of 10"; "burning when I pee"; "rash spreading"; "ear pain" with fever under 102; "vomiting" for under 12 hours; "ran out of my medication" (non-insulin) with 0 to 2 days of supply left; "abnormal result" call-back; "new lump"; "dizzy" without fainting |
| **Routine** | **within 24 hours** | "annual physical" or "check-up"; "follow-up"; "refill" with 7 or more days of supply; "referral paperwork"; "form completion"; "reschedule"; "billing question" about a clinical visit; "mild" or "ongoing" symptoms with no change in 7 days or more |

4.7 **Emergent procedure.** On any Emergent trigger:
- (a) Keep the caller on the line. Do not transfer without a warm handoff.
- (b) Tell the caller clearly to call 911, or to hang up and dial it if they are alone with the patient.
- (c) Alert the DCL by the site's red-line extension within 2 minutes.
- (d) For web, portal, or voicemail Emergent contacts, call the patient back immediately. If there is no answer after 2 attempts within 5 minutes, the DCL decides whether to request a welfare check.
- (e) Complete the intake record after the contact, but within 15 minutes of its end.

4.8 **Urgent procedure.** The intake coordinator must notify the DCL within 10 minutes of band assignment. A triage nurse or clinician must make live contact within 1 hours of the clock start.

4.9 **Priority and Routine procedure.** The queue owner for the site must assign the contact to a named staff member at the time of intake. Priority contacts that have not been responded to by the 3-hour mark are escalated to the DCL automatically by MPAS.

4.10 **Missed targets.** Any contact that exceeds its target is flagged "LATE" in MPAS. The site manager must file an incident report on form OPS-IR-02 within 1 business day.

4.11 **Compliance thresholds** (measured monthly per site):
- Emergent: 100%
- Urgent: 98%
- Priority: 95%
- Routine: 95%

A site below threshold for 2 consecutive months must submit a corrective action plan to the Operations Policy Lead within 10 business days.

4.12 **After-hours contacts.** Clocks run on elapsed time and do not pause when a site is closed. Calls received outside site hours route to the Meridian Nurse Line, which operates 24 hours a day and applies this Section.

### 5. Patient Declines to Give an Insurance ID

5.1 A patient's decision not to give an insurance ID must never delay urgency banding, escalation, or a clinical response. For Emergent and Urgent contacts, do not raise insurance until the clinical response is complete.

5.2 For Priority and Routine contacts, staff must follow these steps in order:
- (a) Ask once: "Do you have your insurance card or ID number available?"
- (b) If the patient declines, ask once more for the reason, using this wording: "Can you tell me what's concerning you, so I can help with the right option?" Do not ask a third time.
- (c) Set field 11 to **Declined (INS-DECL)**. Do not enter "None" or "Unknown," because these have different meanings.
- (d) Offer the options in clause 5.3.
- (e) Record the option chosen in the notes field.

5.3 **Options to offer:**
- **Provide later.** The patient may supply the ID by phone or portal up to 48 hours before the appointment. MPAS sends reminders at 72 hours and 48 hours.
- **Self-pay.** Staff give the published self-pay rate for the visit type and book the appointment as self-pay. No deposit is taken at booking. Payment is due at check-in.
- **Meridian Community Care Program.** Staff offer a referral to the sliding-scale program. Applications are processed within 5 business days.

5.4 Staff must not state or imply that the patient will be refused care, that the appointment will be cancelled, or that the visit will cost more if they have insurance.

5.5 If the patient has not supplied the ID by 48 hours before the appointment, the front desk makes one call. If it is still not supplied, the patient signs Financial Responsibility Form MHP-F-212 at check-in and is seen as self-pay.

5.6 An insurance ID, if given, is entered only in the structured insurance field. It must not appear in free-text notes, email, or messaging.

5.7 **Named exceptions:**
- (a) Patients under 18 contacting without a guardian present: staff do not request insurance details. They record "Guardian to be contacted" and proceed to clause 3.4.
- (b) Referral-originated contacts from Harrowgate Occupational Health Services: insurance is verified through the referral batch, not the patient. Do not ask the patient. Set field 11 to "Provided."
- (c) Patients attending a Meridian Community Care Program clinic session: field 11 is set to "Uninsured." Clauses 5.2 to 5.5 do not apply.

5.8 If a patient who declined asks why the information is needed, staff may say: "It lets us check what your plan covers before the visit, so you don't get surprised by a bill."

### 6. Record-Keeping Requirements

6.1 **Timeliness.** The intake record must be created and saved within 5 minutes of the end of the contact. Emergent records are due within 15 minutes (clause 4.7(e)). A record saved after 24 hours is flagged "LATE ENTRY" and requires the site manager's review.

6.2 **Contents.** The record must contain all 12 mandatory fields. For Emergent and Urgent contacts, it must also contain the time the DCL was notified, the name of the clinician who responded, and the time of first response.

6.3 **Accuracy.** Notes must be factual and use the caller's words. Staff must not write opinions or speculation about the patient's character, motives, or credibility.

6.4 **No deletion.** Records may not be deleted. Corrections are made by addendum, which shows the original entry, the new entry, the editor's ID, the timestamp, and a reason code.

6.5 **Retention periods.**

| Record type | Retention |
|---|---|
| Adult intake record | 10 years from last contact |
| Intake record for a patient under 18 | Until the patient turns 21, or 10 years from last contact, whichever is longer |
| Call recording, Routine/Priority/Urgent | 90 days |
| Call recording, Emergent | 7 years |
| Incident reports (OPS-IR-02) | 7 years |
| Signed MHP-F-212 forms | 7 years from date of signature |

6.6 **Audit.** The site manager reviews a random 5% sample of intake records each month, with a minimum of 10 records per site. The review checks: field completeness, band assignment against trigger phrases, response time against target, and correct use of INS-DECL. Results are submitted to the Operations Policy Lead by the 7th of the following month.

6.7 **Band errors.** If an audit finds a band assigned lower than the trigger phrases required, the staff member is retrained within 10 business days. Two or more such errors by the same person in a calendar quarter must be reported to the Director of Clinical Quality.

6.8 **Access.** Intake records are viewable only by staff with a documented need. Staff must not access the record of themselves, family members, or colleagues. MPAS logs every access, and logs are retained for 3 years.

6.9 **Paper and handwritten notes.** Notes taken during a call must be entered in MPAS within the time limits in 6.1 and shredded within 24 hours of entry.

---

### 7. Exceptions and Escalation

7.1 **System downtime.** If MPAS is unavailable, staff use paper form INT-01-D, which has the same 12 fields. Paper forms must be entered into MPAS within 4 hours of system restoration. Banding and response targets are unchanged.

7.2 **Anonymous callers.** If the caller refuses to give a name, staff still assign a band and escalate as required by Section 4. The record is created as "Unidentified" with a system-generated reference number. Hard-stop fields 4 and 5 may be entered as "Withheld."

7.3 **Repeat contacts.** If a patient contacts again within 24 hours about the same issue, the new contact is assigned at least the band of the earlier contact and flagged "REPEAT."

7.4 **Threats or abusive callers.** Staff may end the contact after one warning. The band is still assigned and any Emergent trigger still applies. The site manager is notified the same day.

7.5 **Language access.** If a caller cannot be understood in English, staff connect the telephone interpreter line within 2 minutes. Emergent triggers do not wait for the interpreter. Staff begin clause 4.7 using the caller's tone and the words they can identify.

7.6 **Conflicts with clinician instruction.** If a clinician directs a different band or procedure, staff follow the clinician and record the clinician's name, time, and instruction in the notes.
